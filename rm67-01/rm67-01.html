    Winding

    Step 4 winds by this ledger, and the hollow needle on the gauge shows the reserve left at bedtime once the days repeat.

    1. 1

      Wrist → rotor

      A heavy platinum weight hangs off-centre. Your arm moves; the weight swings and turns.

    2. 2

      Rotor → spring

      Turning one way only (OneWay), it winds the mainspring through a row of small wheels.

    3. 3

      Spring → tick

      The spring lets go slowly. The balance swings 8 times a second, letting the gears move one notch at a time.

    Where the numbers come from
    • RM: Richard Mille's RM 67-01 / CRMA6 specification: 38.70 × 47.52 × 7.75 mm case, 29.10 × 31.25 × 3.60 mm movement, 28,800 vph, about 50 h (±10%), 25 jewels, CuBe balance at 7.5 mg·cm² with a 50° lift angle, platinum rotor with OneWay winding on ceramic ball bearings, function hand at 2 o'clock showing W · D · H, semi-instantaneous date in a vertical aperture at 5 o'clock, grade 5 titanium spline screws.
    • Corrected from the brief: the selector reads W · D · H (there is no N). RM publishes no variable-geometry rotor or adjustable ribs for the CRMA6 (that idea belongs to other RM calibers), so the rib step and slider became OneWay against a both-ways what-if. The RM 67-01 has no seconds hand; the fourth wheel, which turns once a minute, carries a marker dot instead.
    • std.: 20-tooth escape wheel and the 280° → 170° amplitude curve are typical of a Swiss lever movement; RM doesn't publish them for the CRMA6.
    • est.: 1,000 rotor turns for a full wind, 6.5 barrel turns, 0.12 winding turns per step, 16 h a day on the wrist, each activity's steps and extra wrist-motion turns (golf 12,000 steps + 60 turns from swings, skiing 6,000 + 250, tennis 9,000 + 200, sailing 4,000 + 250, racing cars 3,000 + 40), and the both-ways × 1.6 factor are estimates chosen to be plausible. They set how fast things wind, not the timekeeping.
    • Drawn, not measured: the layout of the wheels in the movement, the number of bezel screws, and the strap and dial details follow the watch's look, not a technical drawing.
